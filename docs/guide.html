Project Guide

Your pocket robotics lab

A little AI that lives on your own machines. You talk, type, or show it a photo, and a local language model answers — no cloud, no accounts. It also reads the room temperature and lights up a friendly face on a small screen. Three devices, working together over your Wi-Fi.

The three parts

Think of it as a brain, a helper, and a sensor.

The brain · laptop

The station

Runs the AI models (for chat, speech, and vision), the web page you control it from, and the logic that ties everything together.

The helper · Raspberry Pi

The hub

A tiny computer that passes messages between devices, shows the bot's face on a screen, and carries the touch pads, microphone and webcam.

The senses · ESP32

The sensor node

A thumb-sized chip that measures temperature and humidity, and has a small screen, a buzzer and a light it can flash on command.

How one question travels

Follow a question from you to an answer — for example, "what's the temperature?"

  1. You ask

    Press a touch pad and speak, type on the web page, or hold up a photo to the webcam.

  2. The station works out what you mean

    If you spoke, it turns speech into text. It then sorts the question — a chat, a coding question, or "look at this picture" — and picks the model that fits.

  3. It checks the room if needed

    Ask about temperature and the station quietly asks the sensor for a fresh reading, then hands that fact to the AI.

  4. The model answers

    A language model running on your own laptop writes the reply — nothing leaves your home.

  5. You see the answer

    It appears word-by-word on the web page and on the bot's face on the hub screen.

What it can do

Talk to it

Ask by voice from the hub's touch pad, or by the microphone button on the web page.

Show it things

Point the webcam, upload a photo, or use your phone's camera, and it describes what it sees.

Know the room

Live temperature and humidity, shown on the screen and used when you ask about the room.

A living face

The hub screen shows a bot face that reacts — calm when idle, talking when answering, alert when it's too hot.

Control panel

A web page shows every device, lets you press buttons (beep, blink, read temperature) and watch answers stream in.

Developer view

A second page shows exactly how each answer was made, the live message traffic, and the health of every machine.
